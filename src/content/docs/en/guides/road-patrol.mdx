Starting at the north gate of Aldermark, four guards — **Garrick**,
**Brenna**, **Osric**, and **Halda** — patrol in day and night pairs,
clearing [estate fences](../estates/#wooden-fences-protocol-v57), furniture,
and player houses built on top of public roads (protocol v156, extended to
furniture and houses in protocol v163). Placing any of these is still
unrestricted — only something that actually blocks the road gets torn down.
In winter, the same pairs also shovel snow off the road as they walk their
route (protocol v163).

## What counts as blocking

The server decides this on its own; you don't need to report anything. An
estate fence or ground-level furniture piece counts as a violation when its
center sits within **3m** of a worldgen road's centerline; a player house
counts if any of its floor cells has its center within that 3m. Anything an
operator placed outside any estate, or a public house whose owner isn't a
player account, doesn't count. Clearing is a guard-only `PatrolSweep`
action — clearing several segments under the same account in one sweep
still counts as **one violation**.

Enforcement is immediate from the moment the server boots — there's no
grace period. (There used to be a 3-game-day warning window after a
restart; it was removed on 2026-11-18.)

## Violations and buy-back

- The **first** violation gets your fence or furniture impounded. Pay half
  its base value to guard captain **{karl.npcName}** and you get everything
  back.
- The **second** violation onward is forfeit — no buy-back.
- Every clearing sends the owner a letter signed by Karl.

Right-click Karl and choose "Find impounded items" to open the buy-back
window, or just ask him in words — his LLM recognizes the request. If
you're standing near him it opens right away; if you're too far, Karl just
gets a note that you asked.

## Furniture and houses

Since protocol v163, the road patrol also clears estate furniture and
player houses blocking a public road, under the same first-offense/
impound, later-offense/forfeit rule as fences:

- An impounded furniture piece comes back as its item; an impounded house
  comes back as its building scroll. Whatever a chest held is returned
  untouched at no extra fee, and a house's floors are cleared along with
  it — the terrain is restored to its original height, the same as fence
  removal.
- Impounded furniture and houses are held for one turn of Serin — **20
  game days**, about **60 real hours** — before an unclaimed impound is
  forfeited. Reclaiming hands over as much as your bag weight and purse
  allow in one trip; anything left over waits for another visit.
- Anyone caught inside an impounded house is moved to town.

## Shovelling snow

Also since protocol v163, the same patrol pairs clear snow off the road as
they walk it: when snow is lying (over 15% cover, or forced snow) within 8m
of the road, a guard clears roughly 5m either side of itself, then walks
10m toward the next waypoint before clearing again. A stretch already
cleared within the last 200 game minutes is skipped. A guard holds a snow
shovel while clearing, and a cleared stretch stays bare for 240 game
minutes (30 real minutes) before snow gradually returns. It's purely
visual — it doesn't change movement speed or cold exposure.

The route runs along Aldermark's north road, out past the Skeleton Crypt
fork to the Thieves' Den fork and back — about **1,145m** round trip, taking
roughly 2 game hours per lap. A lap interrupted (say, by the guard dying)
picks back up where it stopped; guards respawn at the guard station.

## The patrol guards

All four are level 50 with 700 max HP and Guard 24. Since protocol v163
they all carry a Watchman's Spear, which thrusts two-handed instead of
slashing. Garrick and Osric still wear plate armor and a wool cape printed
with the Aldermark crest (protocol v157); Brenna and Halda instead wear a
six-piece modular watch outfit — kettle hat, hauberk, breeches, gloves,
boots, and a tabard that takes dye and a family emblem like a cape (the
kettle hat hides any hairstyle underneath). Karl, back at the guard
station, wears the same watch outfit. A guard's name shows their **rank**
in place of a family name: Karl is captain, Garrick and Osric are
sergeants, and Brenna and Halda are guards.

Karl, Brenna, and Halda also offer their own watch outfit (helmet, hauberk,
breeches, gloves, boots, and tabard — not the spear) for sale or as a gift
the way Rica does with her shopkeeper outfit, priced at 25,000 copper (see
the [update log](../../updates/) for protocol v162 and v163).

Guards keep their weapon sheathed while walking. If a partner or player
within 20m gets into a fight with a monster, they join in against that
monster; within 40m, they run over to help. The weapon is sheathed again
10 seconds after the last hit.

Before clearing anything, the guard assigned to it walks up to the nearest
target and swings their weapon three times first, then clears everything
qualifying within 24m at once (protocol v158) — it isn't instant just
because a guard walked past.

## Breaking out when penned

When a patrolling guard's movement gets stuck, it sends `PatrolBreakOut`
(protocol v160). The server floods outward from the guard's cell over every
cell it can walk to, treating doorways as open:

- Reaching a worldgen road, or getting **48 cells** away, means it isn't
  penned — nothing gets torn down.
- If the flood runs out of room on every side, every estate fence *and*
  estate furniture ringing that area is forfeited (furniture that's a map
  object, like village or inn pieces, doesn't count). Nested rings get torn
  through up to **8 layers** deep in one request.
- This seizure isn't covered by the [buy-back rule](#violations-and-buy-back)
  — it's forfeit outright, no buy-back — but the violation count still goes
  up by one and Karl's letter still goes out.
- A guard standing on furniture (a bed, a chair) doesn't count as penned,
  since the furniture itself walls in every side of its own cell; nothing
  gets torn down and the guard isn't sent back. A guard boxed into one
  cell entirely by furniture also hits this check, but the movement rule
  that normally yields to a trapped mover lets it step out of that cell
  anyway.
- If the guard is still penned after its fences are cleared (say, walled in
  by a house or an operator-placed fence), it's teleported straight back to
  town.
- Failing to reach the next patrol waypoint **three times** in a row sends
  `recall` instead, which sends the guard back to town regardless of
  whether it's penned; reaching one waypoint resets the count to zero.
  Scheduled moves like meals and sleep never trigger this.
