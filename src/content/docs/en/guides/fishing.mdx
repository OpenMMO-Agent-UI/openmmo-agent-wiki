Fishing is the game's first gathering profession and the first user of the
**trained skill** system. Every timer, roll and outcome lives **on the server**;
the client renders and responds.

Species and price tables are in [Fishing data](../../database/fishing/). This
page is about **how to actually fish.**

## The loop

```
Cast ─► Casting (1 s) ─► Waiting (4–12 s, shortened by skill)
                              │ the bite rolls species, size and trophy
                              ▼
                         Bite (2.5 s + 0.5 s latency grace)
                         │ hook in time     │ too late / never
                         ▼                  ▼
                       Fight              Escaped
            (continuous tension tug-of-war)
                         │ exhaust it, then reel  │ line snaps / hook thrown
                         ▼                        ▼
                       Caught                  Escaped
```

## Getting set up

Buy a **{itemName('fishing_rod')}**
({formatCoins(itemsById.get('fishing_rod').basePrice)}) from Rica and
**equip it in your main hand**.

:::note[Rods never come from dungeon chests]
They are bought tools, not endgame loot. Chests hold armor and accessories only.
:::

## Casting

Click at water. You need:

- A fishing rod in your main hand
- To be on the **overworld** (no fishing in dungeons)
- A target within **8 metres**
- Actual **water** — the surface has to sit more than 0.1 m above the bed

**Both ocean and rivers work.** A river's surface sits above its own bed even
high in the hills, so inland rivers cast fine. On land the water surface
collapses below the terrain, so clicking dry ground just walks you there.

## Waiting and the bite

The wait is **4–12 seconds** uniform, shortened **2% per fishing level** (never
below half the minimum).

On a bite the float dips hard. You have **2.5 seconds** to hook, plus **0.5
seconds** of latency grace — judged against the **server's own clock**, so a
laggy-but-in-time click is never punished and a hacked client can't stretch the
window.

:::caution[Don't hook early]
Hooking *before* the bite scares the fish off. Wait for the float to go down.
:::

**To hook**: `SPACE`, any click, or a wheel flick.

## The fight

Hooking is only the start. The fight is a continuous tug-of-war the server
simulates on a **250 ms tick** (four times a second).

The fish alternates between two states:

| State | Duration | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **Running** | 2–3.5 s (longer for rarer fish) | Pulling hard, tension climbing |
| **Resting** | 0.8–2 s | Catching its breath, less pressure |

Most of the fight is spent running, so most of the time you are under pressure.

### Three stances

Switch at any time:

| Stance | How | Effect on tension |
|---|---|---|
| **REEL** | Hold the button / hold `SPACE` / wheel down | **+14/s** |
| **GIVE LINE** | Hold the button / hold `S` / wheel up | **−44/s** |
| **HOLD** | Do nothing | Natural decay only |

`ESC` reels in and gives up.

### Tension — the thing that snaps

The gauge on screen. **It snaps at 100 and the fish is gone.**

| Source | Change |
|---|---|
| Starting tension on the hook-set | **30** |
| A running fish pulls | **(20 + 2 × rarity) / s** |
| ↳ scaled up to **×1.3** by how much line is out | |
| ↳ reduced 1% per fishing level, **capped at 30%** | |
| Reeling | **+14 / s** |
| Natural decay while the fish rests | **−8 / s** |
| Giving line | **−44 / s** |

**The starting tension of 30 is deliberately hot** — leave an unanswered run
alone and you're out of the safe range in about a second and snapped a few
seconds later. You have to react immediately.

:::tip[Giving line always beats the pull]
Giving line sheds 44/s, **more than the strongest pull** (a Golden Sturgeon with
maximum line out manages 39/s). So when the gauge is about to blow, giving line
always brings it down — at the cost of more line out, which raises the
multiplier on the next run.
:::

**Reeling adds 14/s against a resting decay of only 8/s**, so **you can't just
hold the reel down.** That's deliberate: it forces you to alternate between
winding in and bleeding off pressure.

### Stamina — what you're actually depleting

**Stamina isn't on the gauge**, but you can read it from the splash — smaller
splash means a tired fish.

Stamina pool: `38 + 12 × rarity`

**Only drag burns it**, and two conditions must hold: the fish is **running**,
and tension is **at least 20**.

```
drain per second = 2 + 12 × (tension / 100)²
```

That **square** is the heart of the system:

    const drain = 2 + 12 * (t / 100) ** 2;
    const secs = (pool) => `${(pool / drain).toFixed(0)} s`;
    return {
      tension: t, drain: `${drain.toFixed(2)}`,
      minnow: secs(38 + 12 * 1), trout: secs(38 + 12 * 3), sturgeon: secs(38 + 12 * 5),
    };
  })}
/>

**A Golden Sturgeon takes 32 seconds at tension 30 and 8 seconds at tension 90 —
four times the difference.**

And that table only counts **time actually under pressure** (fish running,
tension over the threshold), so real fights take longer because the fish rests.
More importantly: **a slack line lets a resting fish recover stamina** — playing
timid isn't just slow, it loses ground.

### So the correct way to play

**Ride the gauge high without blowing it.** Concretely:

1. Tension starts at 30 after the hook-set, and the fish usually runs
   immediately.
2. **Reel** to push tension up into the 80–95 band.
3. Watch the gauge; as it approaches 100, **give line** to bleed off (−44/s
   brings you back inside in about a second).
4. Around 60–70, stop giving line and start reeling again.
5. When the fish **rests**, that's your chance to take distance — the pull is
   weak and reeling is fast.

The common beginner mistake is **giving line the whole fight to stay safe** —
tension sits at 20–30, stamina barely moves, the fish recovers while resting,
and three minutes later you're nowhere.

### Distance and the endgame

Distance isn't shown as a number — **the float is the fish.**

| Situation | Reel speed |
|---|---|
| Fish resting | 1.6 m/s |
| Fish running | 0.6 m/s |
| Fish exhausted | 2.5 m/s |

Runs take 1.1–1.5 m/s of line, but the fish **never leaves a 6-metre radius**
around the cast point.

At 0 stamina the fish goes **Exhausted** — reel it to within 0.3 m of the line
floor and it lands.

:::caution[Don't drag a lively fish in]
A fish with stamina left, dragged within 1 metre of the floor, **panics into a
fresh run.** Empty the stamina first.
:::

## What breaks a session

**Aborts it**: moving, attacking, disconnecting, dying, stowing the rod
(unequipping it or swapping a weapon into the main hand), or stopping
deliberately.

**Doesn't**: changing a hat, holding an off-hand torch — gear changes that leave
the rod in hand don't break concentration.

## Fishing levels

Catches grant **fishing XP**: `10 × rarity²`

  placeholder="Search catches…"
  columns={[

    { key: 'rarity', label: 'Rarity', align: 'center' },
    { key: 'xp', label: 'Fishing XP', align: 'right' },
  ]}
  rows={items
    .filter((i) => i.catchWeight !== null)
    .sort((a, b) => b.rarityTier - a.rarityTier)
    .map((i) => ({
      name: i.name,
      rarity: i.rarityTier === 0 ? 'Flotsam' : `★${i.rarityTier}`,
      xp: i.rarityTier === 0 ? '0' : 10 * i.rarityTier ** 2,
    }))}
/>

- A hooked fish that **escapes** still consoles you with **2** XP. Escapes never
  reveal what it was — a varying consolation would leak the hidden roll.
- Flotsam is rarity 0, so it grants **nothing**.
- **Fishing grants no character XP** — combat balance is untouched.

### What levels buy you

| Effect | Per level | Cap |
|---|---|---|
| Shorter waits | −2% | Half the minimum |
| Rare species weighting | +3% per point of rarity | None (multiplicative, never inverts the order) |
| Pull you absorb | −1% | **−30%** |
| Reel speed | +1% | — |

**Level 10** unlocks River Salmon, **level 20** the Golden Sturgeon. Below that
they don't appear at all.

## AI agents can fish too

The whole mechanic is **deliberately sized for both humans and agents** — the
2.5-second window is comfortable for an agent-client's network round trip, and
the fight simulation only advances four times a second, so being fast confers no
real advantage.

The desktop client's automatic mode hooks its own bites and plays each stance
beat itself; the LLM only decides whether and where to fish.
