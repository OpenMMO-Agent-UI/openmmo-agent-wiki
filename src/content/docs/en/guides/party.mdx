A party holds up to **5 people** and runs on a **leader** — only the leader
can invite, kick, or hand over the lead.

## Inviting

In chat:

```
/party <name>
```

- The target gets an **invite toast** and has **30 seconds** to answer
  before it expires.
- The inviter can have **up to 5** pending invites at once; more get
  refused.
- A full party (5 members) refuses both new invites and an accept on an
  existing one.
- The first person to accept forms the party (the inviter becomes leader
  automatically); every invite after that just adds to the existing party.

## Checking the roster and party chat

- `/party` with no arguments lists the current roster in chat; the
  leader's name is tagged `(leader)`.
- `/p <message>` speaks only on the party channel — nobody outside the
  party sees it.

## Leader powers: kick and handover

```
/party kick <name>
/party leader <name>
```

- Only the leader can use these; anyone else gets refused with a reason.
- Targeting yourself with `kick`, or targeting someone not in your party
  with either command, is refused with its own reason.
- The kicked member gets a system message; everyone else sees "X was
  removed."
- Handing over the lead doesn't touch the roster — it just moves the
  leader flag to the named member, and the whole party is notified.

## Leaving and disbanding

```
/party leave
```

- An ordinary member leaving doesn't affect the rest of the party.
- **The leader leaving (or disconnecting) doesn't disband the party** —
  the earliest-joined remaining member automatically becomes the new
  leader.
- The party **auto-disbands** once it would drop to **1 member**, and that
  last member gets a "party disbanded" system message.

## The party panel

The party panel shows every member's **HP / max HP / class** live — the
server pushes any party whose health changed once a second, so there's
nothing to refresh manually. Party members' current positions also show
on the world map; the server pushes an update whenever one moves, rather
than the client polling for it.

## Kill XP sharing

Killing a monster doesn't just pay out to whoever lands the finishing blow.

- Any party member **alive on the killer's floor and within 150m** is
  eligible and shares in the kill.
- The XP pool gains **+25%** per eligible member beyond the first; the
  boosted total is then **split equally** among every eligible member
  (killer included), floored, with a **guaranteed minimum of 1**.
- With two or more eligible members, each share is **guaranteed not to
  exceed** what a solo kill would pay — padding the party can't beat
  soloing.
- With only 1 eligible member (or no party at all), XP is exactly the same
  as a solo kill.
- If different parties (or soloers) land hits on the same monster, its kill
  XP now splits by each side's share of the damage dealt instead of all
  going to whoever lands the finishing blow (2026-10-28) — everyone who dealt
  damage gets at least 1 XP. A party's damage is pooled into one share first,
  then split within the party by the rules above.

## Kill loot ownership

(2026-11-01) The weapon, drops, and dungeon key a monster leaves behind now belong to
whoever dealt the most damage over the fight (ties go to whoever hit it first), not
whoever landed the finishing blow. Unlike the XP rule above, party damage **isn't**
pooled first here — this is purely per-character. Loot from a broken barrel, crate,
or clutter chest belongs to whoever broke it.

- Once an item hits the ground, the player with first claim gets a **30-second**
  exclusive pickup window before anyone else can take it.
- The server tells clients who holds the claim through the ground item's
  `claimed_by` field, and sends `GroundItemClaimEnded` once the 30 seconds run out.
- Trying to pick up someone else's claimed loot gets the same head-shake refusal as
  an overweight bag, naming whoever has first claim — the client won't grab it and
  quietly put it back.
- The kill log line now names whoever dealt the most damage, not whoever landed the
  finishing blow.

This fixes loot going to whoever landed the finishing blow even when they weren't the
one who actually won the fight (issue #199).

## Party loot sharing

(2026-11-06)

```
/party loot
/party loot shared
/party loot priority
```

- The default is "priority" mode — the same rule as Kill loot ownership above: only
  the top damage dealer can pick up during the 30-second exclusive window. `/party
  loot` with no argument toggles between the two modes; you can also set `shared` or
  `priority` directly.
- Only the leader can change it; anyone else gets refused with a reason.
- Switching to "shared" doesn't remove the 30-second exclusive window or the
  `claimed_by` lock — it widens who's allowed through it to include every online
  party member, not just whoever holds the claim. Switching back to "priority"
  restores the original one-person rule.
- `/party` with no arguments now also shows whether the party is set to `loot:
  shared` or `loot: priority`.

## The summon scroll

Using a **Scroll of Party Summon** from your bag sends a call to **every
other online party member**, each getting their own accept/decline toast
that expires after **30 seconds**.

- If a member already has an unanswered summons from you out, reading the
  scroll again doesn't send a second one — it waits for the first to
  expire or be answered.
- Accepting teleports you to the caster's side; the summons is refused if
  either the caster or the answering member is in combat or already
  defeated (the same rule `/escape` follows, so a summons can't double as
  a free way out of a fight).
- Rica sells the scroll alongside her other scroll-type stock — see
  [Items](../../database/items/).
