---
title: Weather
description: How regional rain forms, how often it happens, and how the screen, lighting and sound change while it's raining.
---

Weather shipped in protocol v70. Rain is **regional** — the world is baked
into a handful of climates, each climate is cut into a few "sectors," and
each sector independently decides when it rains and for how long. Only the
ground under a rain cell gets dimmed, wet and loud — the whole map doesn't
change at once. **As of protocol v102**, cells slowly drift with the
seasonal wind and grow or shrink over their life (see "Wind and lee" below),
but the drift is slow enough to stay imperceptible — even the smallest,
fastest coastal shower takes about 5 real minutes for its edge to cross a
fixed point, large cells up to 30, and a player walking at 3 m/s can still
outrun one.

## How climates are assigned

Every plot gets a fixed climate baked from the world's terrain data at
generation time — it's not random and it never changes mid-game:

| Climate | Rough rule |
|---|---|
| Wet coast | within 1 km of the sea and below 900 m elevation |
| Alpine | 1,500 m elevation or higher (worldgen paints permanent snow from 1,800 m up) |
| Temperate | everywhere else on land |

The sea never gets rain. **As of protocol v102**, the standalone "rain
shadow" climate is gone, replaced by the dynamic "Wind and lee" check below
— leeward spots get less rain, but it's no longer a fixed zone on the map.

## How often, and for how long

Each climate's sectors roll independently for when it rains, for how long,
how wide, and which way it drifts. Roughly what it feels like (**chances
were halved as of protocol v102** — wet coast and alpine were near 90%,
too much of a season spent under rain):

| Climate | Average frequency | Rain duration | Cell radius | Drift (m/game min) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wet coast | about every 2.6h | 21–30 min | 1.6–2.6 km | 5–9 |
| Temperate | about every 10h | 15–30 min | 3.5–5.4 km | 8–14 |
| Alpine | about every 5h | 22–30 min | 3.3–5.2 km | 6–12 |

Cell edges are sharp — rain is at full strength out to 70% of the radius and
fades to zero over the outer 30%, rather than bleeding out gradually the way
real rain does. Coastal showers drift slowest, so they stay near the shore
they formed on; temperate and alpine cells move faster.

The world map doesn't show cells or a forecast yet — that layer has been
prototyped and works, but it's held back until there's a real gameplay
reason for players to check the weather ahead of time.

## Wind and lee (protocol v102)

Where a cell drifts is set by the **seasonal wind** — fixed at birth, with
the cell moving in a straight line within ±25° of that season's heading:

| Season | Drift heading |
|---|---|
| Winter | east-northeast (22.5° north of east) |
| Spring | turns through north |
| Summer | west-northwest (22.5° north of west) |
| Autumn | turns through south |

Winter and summer aren't opposites — the summer wind comes from the
east-southeast, so it crosses the central Valdran massif before reaching
Aldermark, and rain totals on either side of the massif swap between the two
seasons. Each sector's bake also records the highest terrain ridge in 16
headings within 14 km upwind; a cell reads the ridge in its own upwind
direction at birth and derives a "lee factor" from it — a fully sheltered
spot keeps only 11% of its base chance, while the windward side keeps its
full chance. This dynamic check replaces both the old fixed rain-shadow
climate and the season multiplier that used to apply only near Aldermark.

## What rain does on screen

The ground under a cell reflects three things in real time, and reverts
immediately once you leave it:

- **Dimmer lighting** — directional light drops up to 50%, ambient and
  environment light up to 25%, at full cloud cover. Full overcast reads as
  "cloudy afternoon," never night.
- **Rain and sound** — ground-level rain particles and splash rings appear,
  along with rain ambience and occasional distant thunder, following your
  SFX volume/mute settings and muffled indoors. It never rains indoors or in
  dungeons, and flower petal particles stop spawning while it's raining.
- **Quieter inn music** — bard performances duck through the same
  quiet-zone system rain uses, so the music fades smoothly instead of
  cutting in and out at a cell's edge.

## Getting soaked in the rain (protocol v78)

It's not just wading anymore — **standing in the rain long enough now gets
you wet too**. At full intensity (1.0) it takes 10 in-game minutes (75 real
seconds) of continuous rain to trigger; half intensity takes twice as long.
Intensity at or below 0.02 doesn't accumulate at all. The server checks your
exact position's weather once per second — you don't need to be moving, and
your graphics settings don't matter. An admin-forced rain counts too.

Going indoors (a ground-floor room's actual footprint, not the gaps between
rooms), into a dungeon, upstairs, dying, or loading resets your accumulated
exposure. If you're already wet and keep getting rained on, your remaining
time refreshes back to 450 seconds once it drops below 300 — the same goes
for wading first and then getting rained on. Taking shelter doesn't dry you
off instantly; an active Wet debuff just runs down at its normal rate, or
faster next to a lit campfire.

Rain-triggered Wet has the exact same effect as wading (×0.83 move speed,
×1.5 armor weight) — see [Combat's debuffs](../combat/#debuffs). **As of
protocol v103**, the same precipitation splits into two separately-tracked
amounts depending on whether it's falling as rain or snow — the snow share
counts toward [Cold](#snow-and-cold-protocol-v103), not Wet.

## What rain does to the ground (protocol v78)

- **Puddles** — flat ground gradually grows irregular puddles as rain
  accumulates; at 45% accumulation the ground darkens by 38%. Puddles shrink
  from their edges once rain stops, fully drying within 240 seconds, and
  refill if it starts raining again. Slopes and terrain below sea level
  never puddle, and dungeon floors stay dry.
- **Lit-up rain** — raindrops and ground splashes now pick up the color and
  distance falloff of nearby light sources (torches, campfires); above 0.45
  rain intensity, the sparse light-rain ambience crossfades into the full
  heavy-rain recording.
- **Lightning** (toggleable) — above 0.35 intensity, the sky occasionally
  flashes — the scene lights up for 50ms then fades back to the normal
  day/night lighting, with thunder following 2–5 seconds later, roughly
  every 50–140 seconds. It reads weaker indoors. **Settings → Lightning
  Flashes** is on by default and can be turned off independently of rain
  and thunder audio.

## Snow and cold (protocol v103)

A winter cell has a chance to fall as snow instead of rain — decided once at
birth, and it never switches kind mid-life:

```
snow chance = winter_depth(birth) * max(latitude share(spot z), high(elevation))
```

- `winter_depth` holds at full strength from day 15 to day 75 of winter,
  easing to 0 over half a month into both autumn and spring.
- The latitude share is 0.95 up to z 5,500 (Aldermark sits at z 4,742) and
  eases down to 0.5 by z 8,500 — the southern lowlands still get winter rain
  half the time, not all snow. Sectors above 600–1,200 m elevation snow
  regardless of latitude.
- Shelter checks, cloud darkening, and NPCs ducking indoors all still just
  look at "is it precipitating," regardless of kind; rain and snow are the
  same precipitation total, split into two separately-tracked shares — one
  counts toward [Wet](#getting-soaked-in-the-rain-protocol-v78), the other
  toward Cold below.

Lying snow cover is also a pure function of time — how much snow currently
sits at a point is recomputed by replaying every cell that passed over it in
roughly the last 3,633 game minutes (2.5 game days, about 7.5 real hours) in
5-game-minute steps; nothing needs to be saved. A full snowfall covers the
ground in 90 game minutes (about 11 real minutes); how fast it melts depends
on how cold the spot is — a full cover takes 1,000 game minutes (about 2
real hours) to melt on the coldest winter ground and 180 game minutes (about
22.5 real minutes) on the mildest, at 0.6× speed at night and 1.5× at noon;
switching to plain rain clears it in 150 game minutes (about 19 real
minutes). Seed 42, sampled over 10 years of midwinter (winter days 15–75):

| Point | Snowfall | Any snow on the ground | Cover over half |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aldermark (-1475, 4741) | 18.7% | 75% | 55% |
| Garasden (1929, 2746) | 9.1% | 46% | 27% |
| Riftmark (-2704, 10328) | 7.7% (7.1% rain) | 24% | 12% |
| Southeast (6000, 16000) | 3.5% | 10% | 5% |

**Cold** — triggers from standing in heavy snow (intensity 1) for 20
continuous in-game minutes (75 real seconds ×2 — twice as long as it takes
to get Wet from rain); half intensity takes twice as long, and 0.02 or below
never accumulates. It lasts 450 seconds (1 in-game hour), same duration as
Wet, but tracks separately and can stack with it, multipliers combining:
×0.9 move speed, ×0.9 attack speed, ×1.5 hunger drain, no armor-weight
penalty. Going indoors, upstairs, into a dungeon, dying, or loading resets
the accumulation; if you're already Cold and keep getting snowed on, your
remaining time refreshes to 450 seconds once it drops below 300. The same
campfire that dries off Wet also warms off Cold at the same rate (10 seconds
per real second), and standing near one helps with both at once. See
[Combat's debuffs](../combat/#debuffs) for details.

Admins can force snowfall with `/weather snow [intensity]` — see "Admins can
force the weather" below; the client builds and melts the forced cover
locally at the midwinter pace, since a forced override has no history to
replay.

## NPCs duck out of the rain

Wick (the night-market vendor) and Signe (the town-square performer) both
pack up and head into the inn once rain intensity passes 0.02 at their
usual outdoor spot (Wick sits in chair 42, Signe in chair 39), and resume
their normal schedule once it clears. Sleeping, meals, and other indoor
routines aren't affected.

## Admins can force the weather

Server admins have chat commands — `/weather rain [intensity]`,
`/weather snow [intensity]`, `/weather clear`, and `/weather auto` — to
force server-wide rain, snow, or clear skies, or resume automatic weather,
for debugging or events. Regular players don't see these commands, but
weather forced this way behaves exactly like natural weather for lighting,
sound, and the Wet/Cold debuffs.

## Not in yet

Weather-affected fishing and a forecast layer on the world map are still
missing.
