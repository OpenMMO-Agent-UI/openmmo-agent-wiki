title: Teleportation & bookmarks
description: How random teleport scroll landings are weighted, how bookmarks are saved, and how blessed scrolls and the Ring of Teleport Control aim a teleport at one.
---

A plain **teleport scroll** (`scroll_of_teleportation`) sends you somewhere between 32 meters
and 2 kilometers away, horizontally — outdoors, or on any floor of any dungeon. The east-west
edges follow the world's cylindrical wrap.

## Landing odds

Every square meter of ground you could stand on gets the same weight, wherever it is — your
starting floor, the dungeon's name, whether you've been there before, and your character's level
don't change the odds. The rule is:

> **Chance of landing on a given floor after a successful teleport = that floor's standable area
> ÷ (the outdoor world's standable area + every dungeon floor's standable area)**

Bigger floors come up more often, and the outdoor world dwarfs any single dungeon floor, so
landing in a dungeon is rare. Walls, water, obstacles, stairs and steep slopes don't count as
standable area; buildings only exclude the floor tiles inside a room. Furniture, fences, and
dungeon ground props only block the specific tile they occupy, so a scroll can still drop you in
the middle of a town square. **Monsters don't affect where you land** — a teleport is not
guaranteed to put you somewhere safe.

A scroll is only consumed on a successful landing, and you can read one mid-combat. You can't use
one while dying or while it's listed in a trade. Landing in a dungeon dismounts you, and landing
outdoors dismounts a boat too. Teleport scrolls aren't on any shop's default stock; the shared
world-drop chance is 1%, from monsters, chests and breakable props alike.

## Bookmarks

A **bookmark** saves the spot you're standing on under a name you choose. Each character gets up
to **32 bookmarks**, names run up to **24 characters**, and no two bookmarks on the same character
can share a name, case-insensitively. Where you can save one follows the same rule as where a
random teleport can land: outdoor ground (never inside a dungeon floor), not inside a room, not
in water, not on an occupied tile, not on a steep slope. You can't bookmark someone else's estate
unless you share a family. The server re-checks that same rule again the instant you teleport to
a bookmark, in case the spot stopped being valid since you saved it.

The web client opens the bookmark window from the world map's bookmark button, or with
`/bookmark`.

## Aiming a teleport at a bookmark

To send a teleport scroll at a bookmark instead of a random landing, you need one of:

- **Blessed Scroll of Teleportation** (`blessed_scroll_of_teleportation`) — a teleport scroll
  carrying the "teleport control" effect. Read it plainly and it still lands randomly like a
  regular scroll; choose a bookmark when reading it and it goes straight there, however far away.
- **Ring of Teleport Control** (`ring_of_teleport_control`) — wearing it lets even a plain
  teleport scroll aim at a bookmark, turning every ordinary scroll you carry into a free blessed
  one. The last chest of [the Garden of Stone](../../database/dungeons/) rolls this ring at 5%.

Aiming requires the scroll you're reading, or something you're wearing, to carry the "teleport
control" effect, and that you're not in combat. If either condition fails, the destination choice
is refused — the scroll isn't consumed, and it doesn't fall back to a random teleport either. If
you have more than one bookmark, reading a scroll that can aim shows you the destination list
first.

## Getting a scroll blessed by Maelis

Maelis, at the chapel, blesses unlocked plain teleport scrolls into Blessed Scrolls of
Teleportation for **5,000 copper apiece**, but only while she's standing at her lectern (whenever
her current schedule entry has no other action assigned). Right-click her nearby to open the
blessing menu; if she isn't at the lectern, she turns the request down on the spot.

## Teleport presentation

Random teleports, the Scroll of Return, the Scroll of Estate Return, and an accepted party summon
all share the same effect: a blue-white pillar of light and motes rises from where you stood, your
character fades out at 0.3 seconds, and the 0.52-second departure effect has to finish before the
actual use request goes out. At the destination, your character reappears 0.18 seconds after the
descending light touches the ground, and the ground ring and afterglow fade out slowly. You can't
move or use another item for the whole span from departure to arrival; dying, using another
teleport, or disconnecting mid-departure cancels the request before it's sent.
