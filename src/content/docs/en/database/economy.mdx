## Currency

There is one currency, stored server-side as a single copper integer and
converted for display:

**1 gold (g) = 100 silver (s) = 10,000 copper (c)**

New characters get **no starting money.** Your first income comes from selling
monster drops. The starting weapon is the **{itemName('worn_iron_sword')}**, and
it deliberately **has no price** — merchants refuse it, which shuts down
rerolling characters to cash out starting gear.

The shop list is **deliberately minimal.** The rule is: only stock the pieces a
tier-1 dungeon does *not* drop reliably, as insurance for players whose rolls
keep failing. Guaranteed drops need no insurance, and higher-tier pieces stay
out to protect the farming loop.

That's why the **{itemName('leather_helmet')}** (Old Crypt's guaranteed drop)
and the **{itemName('iron_sword')}** are **not in the shop**.

### Sold something by mistake?

The last **10** items you sold to a merchant can be bought back for exactly what
you were paid. It's a recovery path for misclicks, and the round trip is
gold-neutral so it doesn't disturb the spread — something you haggled a high
price for costs that much to buy back.

The list is kept per character and per merchant, survives reconnecting, and has
no time limit — but **it is lost when the server restarts.**

## After hours: Wick, the night merchant

Rica goes to bed at night, and **a sleeping merchant won't even open a trade
window** — even one already open gets force-closed. After dark, head to the
Aldermark road and find **Wick** instead: he sits idle at the western edge of
town by day and only sets up shop at night.

Wick sells {wick.catalog.length} things and buys at **{wick.sellRatePercent}%**
of base price (lower than Rica's {rica.sellRatePercent}%):

being out after dark, not a better deal than waiting for morning.

### Merchant stalls (/lay_stall)

Merchants can type `/lay_stall` to spread a table out as a visible sign
they're open for business, and `/pack_stall` to fold it back up (logging out
packs it up too — one stall per merchant, outdoors only).

A stall is no longer just a prop — **clicking someone else's laid-out stall
opens a trade window with them directly**, no invite or accept step needed.
Full rules in [Player trading](../../guides/trade/).

## Haggling

Rica is an LLM-driven NPC, so **you can talk the price down.** All the price
protection sits on the server:

1. The server computes a price band `[min, max]` from the base price, the
   spread and a **CHA modifier**.
2. The LLM can only pick an adjustment inside that band — never an absolute
   price. Anything outside gets clamped or refused.
3. The server guarantees **arbitrage is impossible** — the most discounted buy
   price is always above the highest sell price.
4. There are daily budgets and cooldowns: 1 gold per game day for the merchant,
   40 silver per player, a **30-second** haggle cooldown, and any deal is
   **single-use and expires after 5 minutes.**

Higher CHA widens the band the server allows (roughly ±5 to 25 percentage
points) — a charismatic character really does negotiate better.

Once a deal is granted, the merchant now says it out loud in a chat message
too — the discount or markup, what it's for, how long it lasts — not just
inside the trade window, so you don't have to open trade to learn one is
waiting. Merchants will also occasionally lead with a discount of their own,
without you haggling first.

:::tip
A good story, a piece of news or some judicious flattery genuinely earn a
discount. Being rude can earn a **markup** — it's symmetric, and in character.
:::

### Not interested? Say "not now"

A trade or deal window an NPC pushes at you now has a "Not now" button, and
letting the toast time out unanswered counts as a decline too. Once declined,
**that NPC won't push another offer at you for 10 minutes.**

## Price index (the evening market meeting)

The buy price merchants charge for **consumables** (potions, whetstone oil,
food, scrolls, and the like) drifts slowly with the server's **per-capita
active gold supply**, scaled by a price index between 90% and 200%.
Durable goods like equipment and cape dyes are completely unaffected, and
**sell-back prices don't move either** — only the buy side, otherwise
buying and selling would scale together and cancel the adjustment out.

- Adjustments don't run on a fixed clock — they land after **sunset on the
  day the small moon Serin is fully dark** (a 20-game-day cycle), roughly
  every 2.5 real days, and can fall at any time of day.
- When it's time, Rica and Wick meet up at Rica's shop for a short "merchant
  meeting" scene, and their dialogue hints at which way prices are moving
  and why — eavesdrop to get a jump on the next swing.
- Each meeting moves the index by **at most ±10 percentage points**, and
  the index itself is clamped to 90%–200%.
- The haggling band is calculated **on top of** the price index — the index
  applies first, then your CHA-based bargaining range.

## Non-merchant NPCs

Merchants aren't the only ones who trade. The guard **Karl** has a wishlist and
pays **{karl.wishlistRatePercent}%** of base for what he wants:

      ricaSell: formatCoins(sellPrice(i?.basePrice ?? null, rica.sellRatePercent)),
    };
  })}
/>

**Buying from Rica and selling to Karl turns a profit** — that's intended
content, effectively an emergent delivery quest. It's capped by Karl's wallet
({formatCoins(karl.walletCap)}), so it can't run away. His money comes from a
{formatCoins(karl.salaryPerDay)} per game day salary that stops once the wallet
is full.

Note that he **only buys while the wishlist is unmet** — he doesn't want what's
already in his bag.

Karl's spear and full set of leather armor are **issued gear**, granted and
equipped automatically whenever he comes online rather than being loot — that
gear **can't be sold**, either direction, so logging an NPC in and out can't
be farmed for free equipment.

### NPCs remember you (favor)

LLM-driven NPCs keep a rolling window of recent conversation (the last 30
lines, timestamped to game time), so a back-and-forth stays coherent across
several turns; bards additionally remember the last 8 songs they played, so
they don't repeat themselves.

Conversations also build up a **favor** score, server-side, adjustable by the
LLM up to ±1 per turn and capped at ±5 total. Once a player's favor clears a
threshold, the NPC starts treating them as a regular worth a private deal —
that's the mechanism behind the keepsake system below.

### Keepsakes

Some things a non-merchant NPC carries are **keepsakes** — they never appear
when you browse their stock, and asking to buy one gets you turned down. Only
when the NPC **makes a personal offer** to a specific player can that player
buy one, while the offer lives (a few minutes); a sold keepsake is restocked
after a while. The current example is Signe's gilded mandolin — how to become
a regular she would offer it to is covered in
[Music & the bard](../../guides/music/).

## Where gold comes from

Gold issuance is deliberately controlled. There are only three repeatable
sources:

| Source | Amount |
|---|---|
| Coin piles (dungeon chests) | 1–10 copper |
| Dungeon chest gold | depth × 500 to depth × 1500 |
| Fishing | 8–24 copper expected per catch |

Compare that to the NPC guard's daily wage of
{formatCoins(karl.salaryPerDay)}.

Weapons dropped by monsters are **mostly unsellable "Worn" variants**, on
purpose — if higher-tier weapons cashed out reliably from ordinary monsters
they would be a money printer. Ordinary armed monsters drop at only 10%; only
bosses are guaranteed.
